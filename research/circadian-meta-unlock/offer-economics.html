5. Rules that protect the margin

  1. Written only. Calls are the fastest way to turn a $178-an-hour engagement into a $60-an-hour one. If he wants calls, they are priced separately.
  2. Analyst access only, from UpClick's portfolio. Never admin, never the card, never publishing. The moment UpClick publishes, UpClick owns outcomes and the guarantee becomes uncapped exposure.
  3. The guarantee has conditions and they are written down. Five working days to implement each plan, an agreed spend floor, access from day one. Miss a condition, the guarantee lapses for that month.
  4. Full tier only with clean measurement. Margin figure from Jacob, Shopify Meta channel at Maximum data-sharing, and a baseline month on record before the share applies. No margin figure, no Full tier: Core becomes the top.
  5. Sell the Review, then let the Review sell the Sprint. The report's last page is the Sprint. Do not pitch the Sprint before the report exists; the report is where the numbers come from.
  6. Separate invoice line from Kris. Same UpClick invoice from month four, separate line, so either engagement can end without touching the other.

6. Decision

Recommend

Offer all three. Lead the email with the Sprint. Position the Review as the risk-free way in, with the credit. Keep the Full tier as the answer to his profit objection, and drop it silently if he will not share margin. Do not offer an hourly rate or a per-question price; he asked for structure, so sell one thing to buy now and one thing to buy after.

If he takes the Review only, UpClick earns a fair rate for ten hours and gets the diagnosis that makes the next conversation easy. If he takes the Sprint, Circadian roughly doubles as an account for a quarter with no new delivery seat. If he takes Full, UpClick earns slightly less than the Sprint in a normal quarter and more than it only if he scales, which is the right shape of bet for a client whose objection was margin.
