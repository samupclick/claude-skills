# NZ Retirement Village & Aged Care Directory — Feasibility Research

**Prepared:** 25 August 2026
**Question:** Is a new directory website for the NZ retirement home / retirement community space feasible? How much competition exists, and is it worth monetizing?
**Method:** Four parallel deep-research streams (~120 web searches across competition mapping, market sizing, monetization models, and demand/SEO analysis). Facts carry sources; estimates are labelled.

---

## Verdict (TL;DR)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Feasible?** | Yes as a *differentiated wedge*; **no** as another generic listings directory — that layer is saturated. |
| **Competition?** | **Moderate-to-high.** Three entrenched incumbents (Eldernet, Village Guide, Aged Advisor) — but all are small founder-run businesses, and three validated gaps are open. No offshore players operate in NZ. |
| **Worth monetizing?** | **Yes, but capped.** Realistic path to **$150k–$400k ARR in 2–3 years**; ceiling ≈ **$700k–$1M** with category leadership + adjacent revenue. A profitable lifestyle/portfolio business, not a venture-scale one. |

**The play, if you build:** don't launch "another directory." Launch the **pricing-transparency and comparison authority** (the standardized comparison resource Consumer NZ is literally campaigning for), built on official Retirement Villages Register disclosure data, structured for AI-search citation — and let the directory grow underneath it. Timing is unusually good: the Retirement Villages Act reform bill lands ~mid-2026, village sales have slowed (operators are hungry for qualified buyers), and AI search is reshuffling who gets cited. One caveat: an anonymous programmatic site (compareretirementvillages.co.nz) began attacking exactly this gap in ~2025–26.

## 1. The market

### Retirement villages (the money side)

- **491 villages, ~43,600 units, 50,791–53,000+ residents** (JLL NZ Retirement Village Database YE Dec 2024; RVA 2026). Register counts range ~490–540 depending on definition. ([JLL](https://www.jll.nz/en/trends-and-insights/research/retirement-villages-market-review), [RVA](https://www.retirementvillages.org.nz/), [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/companies/aged-care/retirement-village-population-now-totals-50791-residents-new-study-shows/KRS6GVCHWVAYNLKWBOHRNSEXSM/))
- **~130 people move into a village every week** (~6,800/yr); penetration is **14.3% of the 75+ population** and rising ([RVA](https://www.retirementvillages.org.nz/), JLL).
- **Pipeline:** 211 villages in development (~21,500 units); projected supply **shortfall of 8,367 units by 2033 / 23,302 by 2048**; village population forecast ~68,000 by 2028 ([The Weekly Source](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/nz-will-need-26000-retirement-village-units-in-next-decade-jll-report/), [Retirement Commission](https://retirement.govt.nz/news/latest-news/demand-rising-for-retirement-village-units)).
- **Demographics:** 65+ population hits **1,000,000 by 2028**; 85+ grows from 93,000 (2022) to **251,000–305,000 by 2048** ([Stats NZ](https://www.stats.govt.nz/news/one-million-people-aged-65-by-2028/)).
- **Unit economics:** occupation right agreements (ORA), median advertised price **$691k** (Village Guide, mid-2026), median *lowest* entry price per village **$490k**; deferred management fee **20–30%** (155 of 228 villages cap at exactly 30%); median weekly fee **$179**, published exactly by only 27 of 233 villages ([Village Guide](https://www.villageguide.co.nz/industry-insights), [compareretirementvillages.co.nz](https://compareretirementvillages.co.nz/guides/retirement-village-costs-nz/)).

### Aged residential care (the coverage side)

- **~670 facilities, ~41,500 beds** across rest home / hospital / dementia / psychogeriatric levels; ~$2.7B/yr government funding ([Treasury case study](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2024-05/pc-inq-is-case-study-aged-residential-care.pdf), [NZ Herald/Listener](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/the-listener/new-zealand/aged-care-crisis-too-few-beds-rising-costs-and-a-failing-health-system/MVXSJQG7VZE57MROWKWAO4F7RY/)).
- **Bed shortage, not vacancy problem:** ~1,260 beds closed in the last year; projected **shortfall of ~12,000 beds by 2032** ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/514510/danger-nz-will-be-12-000-residential-care-beds-short-by-2032-report-finds)). Entry runs through NASC needs assessment and hospital discharge — placement waits of 92–219 days in some regions. **Most care homes don't need demand generation.** The monetizable slice is premium rooms and ORA-based care suites.

### Who could actually pay

- **Big 6** (Ryman, Summerset, Oceania, Metlifecare, Arvida, Bupa): 46–47% of villages but **63–67% of units** — they run their own sales engines and treat directories as marginal.
- **Independents hold 54.3% of village sites** and ~50% of ARC facilities. Deduplicated by operator, the paying universe is roughly **350–500 organizations**, with the realistic revenue core being **~150–250 independent / mid-tier operators** who lack in-house marketing.
- **Market conditions 2024–26 favour a lead-gen pitch:** village sales slowed with the housing market (median advertised price down from its Oct 2024 peak of $718k), Ryman posted successive losses ($513M FY25, $171M FY26) and suspended dividends, Oceania carried **$227M of unsold development stock**, Ryman's flagship The Helier was reported 69% empty. Operators are fighting for fewer buyers — which is exactly when they pay for qualified enquiries.

## 2. The competition

### Tier 1 — the three real incumbents

| Site | Profile | Moat | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eldernet** (eldernet.co.nz + retirementvillages.co.nz) | Founded **1997**, Christchurch; still 100% founder-owned (Eleanor Bodger; family business — *not* acquired). Covers both villages (534 properties / 487 villages) and all residential care. | The **only real-time aged-care bed vacancy data in NZ**; 29-year brand; "Where From Here" printed handbooks distributed free through libraries/health agencies ("the older person's bible"); ~29k visits/month. Platform rebuild underway 2025–26. | Weaker than Village Guide on consumer village SEO; dated utility feel. |
| **Village Guide** (villageguide.co.nz) | Launched 2015 by Paula Bishop; founder-owned; ~5-person operation. Villages + rest homes. | **The consumer SEO winner** — #1 directory on most "[city] retirement villages / rest homes" queries; comparison tables built from disclosure statements; lead-gen + sponsored-content model; publishes industry price data. | Solo-owner scale; comparison data partial; operator-funded, so structurally soft on fee transparency. |
| **Aged Advisor** (agedadvisor.nz) | Founded ~2015 by Nigel Matthews, Christchurch. Reviews across villages + care. | **Owns the review intent outright** (5 of 6 top results for "retirement village reviews nz"); annual NZ's Best Awards (algorithm co-developed with University of Canterbury). | Only ~1,100 reviews across ~300 facilities in a decade; dated UX; effectively a one-person business. |

### Tier 2 — official/free infrastructure

- **Retirement Villages Register** (Companies Office): the statutory dataset — every village + disclosure statements. Zero consumer UX. *This is the free raw material for the wedge.*
- **Sorted / Te Ara Ahunga Ora Retirement Commission**: high-trust education, no directory.
- **Health NZ / MoH certified-provider lists + audit reports**: open data, buried in PDFs; no consumer site fuses audits + reviews + vacancy + pricing.
- **Healthpoint** (acquired 2024 by Jonas/Constellation Software): clinician-oriented ARC listings, funded by provider/government contracts. Watch item, not consumer competitor.
- **RVA member finder; Aged Care Association listings; Seniorline** (Health NZ phone service).

### Tier 3 — property portals

Trade Me Property, realestate.co.nz and OneRoof all run retirement-living categories (unit listings only — no care info, fees or reviews). They own the "buy a villa" moment, not the research journey.

### Tier 4 — new/niche entrants (the interesting signal)

- **compareretirementvillages.co.nz** — anonymous operator, live ~2025–26, mines Companies Office disclosure statements across **233 villages**; publishes real fee/DMF/entry-price data; **already ranks #1 for "compare retirement villages nz"** and top for cost queries. Textbook programmatic-AEO play, but no named brand, no E-E-A-T, no visible accountability. *Validates the gap; beatable on credibility.*
- **seniorsavings.co.nz, silvercare.org.nz, seniorguide.co.nz network** — affiliate/SEO content plays.
- **Content interlopers ranking on money terms:** Canstar (#1 "best retirement villages nz"), MoneyHub, a *moving company's* listicle (Wisemove), a home-care company (HomeCarers) and even a single village's blog (Longridge) — a lone village's post outranks every directory nationally for cost queries. **This is how thin the money/education SERPs are.**
- **Dead/absent:** findaretirementvillage.co.nz (DNS dead). **No offshore entrant** — A Place for Mom, Lottie, carehome.co.uk, DPS/agedcareguide (AU) have zero NZ presence.

### SERP reality check (live probes, Aug 2026)

- "[city] retirement villages": **operators dominate** (Ryman, Summerset, Metlifecare, Oceania, Arvida, Bupa); usually one directory slot.
- "rest homes [city]": directory-led (Village Guide #1, Eldernet #2–3, Aged Advisor mid).
- "reviews": Aged Advisor owns it. "Best": Canstar #1.
- "compare / costs / fees": thin and newly contested — compareretirementvillages.co.nz, sponsored NZ Herald stories, home-care blogs.
- "retirement village vacancies nz": **returns job ads** (SEEK/Jora, 7 of 7) — a completely unserved intent.

**Competition intensity: saturated** on generic listings, unit-for-sale listings, and "what is a village" editorial. **Genuinely open:** (1) pricing/contract transparency, (2) urgent care-placement journey + bed availability (Eldernet's monopoly, single point of attack), (3) fusing open audit data with reviews, (4) AI-citable answer content.

## 3. Monetization

### How this category makes money elsewhere

| Model | Example | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| **Commission per move-in** | A Place for Mom (US) | 85–100% of first month's rent; **US$3,500–12,000 per placement**; ~US$620M revenue — and a Senate probe, steering accusations, review-manipulation exposés. Lottie (UK, US$21M Series A) runs per-lead → commission + SaaS. |
| **Freemium subscription** | carehome.co.uk (UK) | £990/yr Enhanced, £1,980/yr Platinum (+VAT) ≈ NZ$2,200–4,400. Autumna: flat £58/mo, explicitly anti-referral-fee. |
| **Flat promotional listing** | villages.com.au (DCM, AU) | **A$1,000/12 months** (≈NZ$1,100); capped banner ads; part of a B2B media ecosystem (newsletter, events, training). |
| **Concierge placement fee** | CareAbout (AU) | Flat fee paid by provider on successful placement only. |
| **Print + digital hybrid** | DPS Aged Care Guide (AU, acquired by CarePage); Eldernet (NZ) | Directory + printed regional guides carrying provider advertising. |

### What NZ incumbents actually charge

All four NZ players run **subscription / featured-placement / sponsored-media** models (Eldernet adds print; Healthpoint adds government contracts). None publish rate cards — pricing is sales-negotiated (a mystery-shop email would close this gap). None run per-move-in commission. Total NZ sector spend on directories is plausibly **under ~$2M/yr, split three ways** — each incumbent is viable only as a small founder-run business.

### What a lead is worth in NZ

- **Village lead:** a $700k ORA at 25% DMF ≈ **$175k eventual DMF revenue** per resident, plus ~27% development margin on new-builds. US operators pay $3.5k–12k per move-in for a cheaper product. A settled NZ move-in defensibly justifies **$5k–15k**; a qualified enquiry, **$50–150** (benchmarked against est. NZ$3–8 CPCs and 5–10% click-to-enquiry conversion on Google Ads).
- **Aged-care lead:** low and falling — government-set prices, beds fill via NASC amid shortage. Only **premium rooms / care suites** (which behave like village sales) monetize, plus a chronic recruitment-advertising need.

### Realistic revenue model for a new entrant (estimates)

Subscriptions at NZ-plausible $1,000–3,000/yr (avg ~$1,500), against ~600 payable sites:

| Stage | Paying sites | ARR |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1–2 (10% penetration) | 60 | **~$90k** |
| Established (25%) | 150 | **~$225k** |
| Category leader (40%, incl. group deals) | 240 | **~$360k** |

Plus cost-per-lead at maturity (~$54k–$325k/yr at 5k–20k visits/month), display/sponsored (immaterial: $5k–25k). **Ceiling ≈ $700k–$1M ARR** only with category leadership + adjacencies (ORA unit listings at property-portal economics, care-suite leads, recruitment ads, print/events). Commission-per-move-in is the wrong model for NZ: no established norm, in-house salesforces, contested attribution over a 6–18-month journey, and the A Place for Mom reputational record in a two-degrees-of-separation country.

## 4. Demand and the gap

- **Volume is niche, value is high.** No public NZ keyword-volume data exists; modelled aggregate demand is **tens of thousands of searches/month** across the whole keyword universe, head terms in the low thousands. But ~15% of 65+ NZers (~130k people) are considering a village move, 80% expecting to move within five years; research windows run **2.6–3.2 years**; and every decision is researched by the retiree *plus* adult children (45–65) — multiple searchers per transaction, six-to-seven-figure transaction values.
- **The verified pain point is pricing/contract opacity.** Consumer NZ is campaigning for "a brief comparison document… standardised across villages"; the Commerce Commission warned **12 operators (180+ villages)** over Fair Trading Act concerns (Feb 2024); the RV Act review drew 11,000 submissions. Documented horror stories (weekly fees continuing after leaving; ~$95k cost of a 16-month stay; year-long repayment delays) keep this in the news.
- **Regulatory timing:** reform package announced **4 Dec 2025** — mandatory capital repayment within 12 months, interest from 6 months, independent disputes scheme, stronger disclosure — **bill expected mid-2026**. A guaranteed news cycle for exactly the content a transparency site would own.
- **AI search shift:** AI Overviews appear in up to ~55% of searches and cite ~13 sources; outbound clicks drop ~40% when they appear. US senior-living evidence shows families using ChatGPT through the whole journey (one operator group: ChatGPT referrals ×5 in a year). **NZ's citable corpus is thin** — no strong Reddit/UGC layer, incumbents are listing-first not answer-first. The citation slots for "how much does a retirement village cost NZ", "DMF explained", "village vs rest home" are winnable — directly your AEO skillset.

## 5. Recommendation

### Verdict

**Build it only as the transparency/comparison authority — not as directory #4.** As a generic directory the idea fails: saturated listings layer, operator-dominated SERPs, three incumbents already splitting a small pie. As a wedge play it's genuinely attractive *for you specifically*: the build cost is low with programmatic/AEO skills, the raw data is free (statutory register), the pain point is regulator-validated, the news cycle is scheduled (mid-2026 bill), and the only competitor executing the wedge is anonymous with no E-E-A-T.

### The play

1. **Own the money layer:** per-village standardized fee tables (entry price, weekly fee, DMF %, accrual schedule, capital-gain treatment, worked 5/10-year exit-cost examples) from disclosure statements — the thing Consumer NZ wants to exist. Named brand, cited sources, expert review = beat the anonymous incumbent-in-the-gap on credibility.
2. **Structure everything for AI citation** (JSON-LD per village, FAQ/HowTo schema, machine-readable fact tables) — informational clicks are shrinking; citations are the new distribution.
3. **Fix unserved intents:** "retirement village vacancies" (currently job ads), dementia-care local queries (weak competition), audit-report surfacing.
4. **Monetize the village side:** featured placement + qualified-enquiry leads ($50–150) to the ~150–250 independents and mid-tier chains in slow-sales regions; care-suite leads later. Flat, disclosed fees (Autumna's ethical positioning) — **avoid** per-move-in commission.
5. **Treat aged-care listings as coverage/completeness**, not revenue.

### Expectations & exit

- 18–24 months to $150k–300k ARR *if the wedge lands*; this is a lifestyle/portfolio asset, not a venture bet.
- Plausible exit paths exist: this category gets acquired (Healthpoint → Constellation 2024; DPS → CarePage in AU; Caring.com → SilverAssist) — a data-rich NZ transparency asset would be a logical bolt-on for an incumbent, a media buyer, or an AU player entering NZ.

### Key risks

1. **compareretirementvillages.co.nz** has first-mover position in the exact wedge (though anonymous and possibly AI-generated — Google may discount it, or it may entrench).
2. **SEO patience:** incumbents have 10–29 years of links/trust; head terms are effectively closed; you win on long-tail + citations, which takes 12+ months.
3. **Small ceiling:** ~$2M total sector directory spend today; even winning means a modest business.
4. **Operator pushback:** transparency content antagonizes the people you monetize (Village Guide's structural conflict — but also your differentiation).
5. **Eldernet's 2025–26 platform rebuild** could close gaps before you open them.

### Validate before building (1–2 weeks, near-zero cost)

1. Pull NZ keyword volumes (Keyword Planner/Ahrefs, geo=NZ) — the one dataset nobody publishes.
2. Mystery-shop Eldernet, Village Guide and Aged Advisor rate cards (sales email) — closes the incumbent-pricing gap.
3. Run an AI-citation audit: 20–30 NZ retirement prompts across ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini/AI Overviews; record who gets cited today.
4. WHOIS/Companies Office/DNS-history check on compareretirementvillages.co.nz ownership and traction.
5. Call 5–10 independent village operators (slow-sales regions first): "would you pay $150/month for qualified enquiries?" — the entire business case hangs on this answer.

## Method & confidence notes

Compiled from four parallel research agents (~120 searches; ~260 tool calls) on 25 Aug 2026. Direct fetches to many NZ domains were proxy-blocked, so some evidence rests on search-index extracts of primary pages — every load-bearing claim above carries its source. Known gaps flagged for validation: exact NZ keyword volumes, incumbent rate cards, absolute incumbent traffic, compareretirementvillages.co.nz ownership, ARC bed split by care level, and register count discrepancy (491 JLL vs ~540 claimed elsewhere).
