# Course Glossary

Every term taught so far, in plain English. "Remember" is the benchmark figure for this business
(cold US traffic, $27–$57 digital products) worth knowing by heart. New terms are appended as
lessons teach them.

## Taught in Lesson 1.1

| Term | Stands for | Plain meaning | Remember |
|---|---|---|---|
| Media buying | — | Paying ad platforms (here Meta = Facebook + Instagram) to show ads, and managing that spend so it turns a profit | — |
| Impression | — | One display of your ad on one person's screen | — |
| CPM | Cost per mille (mille = thousand) | What Meta charges you per 1,000 impressions. Set by Meta's auction, not by you | ~$30 US (range $25–$50) |
| CTR | Click-through rate | Of the people shown the ad, the % who click it (clicks ÷ impressions) | ~2% is healthy |
| CPC | Cost per click | What one visitor to your page costs. CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR) | ~$1.50 at the above |
| Cold traffic | — | Strangers who have never heard of you (vs "warm": past visitors, email list, buyers) | ~90% of spend here |
| Conversion | — | A visitor completing the action you wanted — in this business, a purchase | — |
| CVR | Conversion rate | % of sales-page visitors who buy (purchases ÷ visitors) | 2–4% cold at $27–$57 |
| CPA | Cost per acquisition | What one *buyer* costs you. CPC ÷ CVR, or total spend ÷ purchases | ~$50 at the above |
| Funnel | — | The buyer's full path: Ad → Sales page → Checkout (with bumps) → Upsells | — |
| Front-end | — | The main product the ad sells; the price on the "front door" | $27–$57 here |
| Order bump | — | A tick-box add-on offer on the checkout page, bought in the same transaction | $17–$37, 20–40% take |
| 1-click upsell | — | An offer shown *after* payment, accepted with one click because the card is already stored (HighLevel does this) | $47–$197, 5–15% take each |
| Take rate | — | % of buyers who accept a given bump or upsell | see above |
| AOV | Average order value | Average total revenue per buyer across front-end + bumps + upsells. Sum of (price × take rate) | ~$60 in our worked example |
| Refund rate | — | % of revenue handed back to unhappy buyers; arrives days–weeks after purchase | 5–10% for info products |
| Leak | — | The share of each revenue dollar lost to costs that scale with sales: processing fees + refunds. The margin is what's left: margin = 1 − leak | ~9–12% here |
| Contribution margin | — | The share of each revenue dollar left after the leak (card processing ~3%, refunds) | ~0.90 (i.e. 90%) |
| ROAS | Return on ad spend | Revenue ÷ ad spend. The number this whole business is steered by, read at 1-day / 7-day / 30-day windows | break-even ≈ 1.11 at 90% margin |
| Break-even ROAS | — | The ROAS at which profit is exactly zero. 1 ÷ contribution margin. Sanity check: always a ratio a little above 1 (typically 1.05–1.4) — never a dollar amount | 1.11 at 0.90 margin |
| Max CPA | — | The most a buyer can cost before you lose money. AOV × contribution margin. Sanity check: always *below* the AOV — you can't afford to pay more for a customer than they bring in | $54.59 in our worked example |
| Cost-per-anything rule | — | Every "cost per X" = money spent ÷ number of X received. Never a price × a rate | CPC = spend ÷ clicks |
| KPI | Key performance indicator | A number a business steers by | here: 1/7/30-day ROAS |
